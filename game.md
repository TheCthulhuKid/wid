The core game boils down to:
- 4d6 as a standard roll. Can go up to 5d6 if assisted. Can go down to 3d6, 2d6, or 1d6 if scared.
- Each applicable item lets a PC add +1 to a die result.
- Applicable expertise adds +1 hit to the results of a roll.

So a dice roller would probably have to let the user input some variables before rolling:
- Assisted?
- Scared, and to what level?
- Rank of tested attribute?
- Any expertise or items apply?

And would output total hits, total misses, total fumbles.
- Hits = 5 or 6; If assisted, 4s count as well.
- Fumbles = equal or under tested attribute.
- Misses = anything that isn't a hit or fumble.

Genre kits would change certain rules, such as the number of dice rolled or the effects of certain items.
## Heckboy
Attributes: Aberrance, Ferocity, Savvy
Roll Adjustments: PCs can use their powers to add more dice, at the cost of strain. A reliable power adds +1d6 to the dice pool, and +1 strain to their results. An erratic power adds +2d6 to the dice pool, and +2 strain to the results.

## Second Contact
Attributes: Science, Culture, Muscle
Roll Adjustments: Items are replaced by Tech. Rather than a flat +1 to a single dice like Items, Tech offers a bonus equal to Science + Culture to a single die, but has limited uses. The bonus depletes by -1 after each use; at 0, the Tech is depleted or broken and can no longer be used.

## Oldheart
Attributes: Depravity, Hospitality, Fitness
Roll Adjustments: After rolling a test, PCs can choose a number of dice to reroll. Each die they reroll gives them +1 Corruption. There's no limit to how many times they can reroll a die, as long as they keep taking Corruption.

## Ball Season
Attributes: Propriety, Stoutness, Yearning
Roll Adjustments: None. The changes are on the difficulty side of the roll.

## This Isn't Happening
Attributes: Instincts, Endurance, Charity
Roll Adjustments: None. The changes involve what you can spend extra hits on.
